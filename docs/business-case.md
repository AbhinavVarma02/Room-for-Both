# Business case

## Who it is for

Independent retail and direct-to-consumer brands with a handful of staff: a small-batch skincare maker, a
ceramics studio, a coffee roaster. They want to reach new customers through a physical partner (a bookstore,
café, gallery, or boutique) but have no partnerships team.

The host side is just as small: an independent venue that would welcome the right partner but has limited
floor space, staff hours, and evenings to give, and its own rules about what it carries.

## The problem

Collaborations between small businesses usually start with a friendly message and stall on details:

- The brand proposes a weekend event; the venue only does weeknights.
- The venue's fee is reasonable, but the brand's own costs push the total over budget.
- One side assumes the other will staff it.
- Nobody knows which option to suggest instead, so the conversation ends.

Both sides lose the time spent, and an opportunity that could have worked in a different shape is dropped.

## What Room for Both does

1. **Finds a culturally grounded reason to talk.** Qloo's taste data suggests venues whose audiences connect with
   the brand's cultural references, and a collaboration theme, with every signal traceable to its source.
2. **Puts both sides' real terms on the table.** The host can answer privately through a link; nothing about the
   host is guessed.
3. **Shows every workable shape at once.** Pop-up, shelf placement, and co-hosted event, at several lengths, each
   marked feasible, conditional, or no-go with reasons.
4. **Shows what would change the answer.** The smallest negotiable change that unblocks a blocked option, checked
   by the same rules, and the first three questions worth asking.
5. **Produces a brief both sides can read.** A printable draft that separates what each party said from what was
   observed, assumed, or still unknown.

## Value hypothesis

These are hypotheses to test with users, not measured outcomes:

- **Fewer dead-end conversations:** a no-go is replaced by a specific alternative or a specific question.
- **Faster first meetings:** both sides arrive with the same structured facts.
- **Fairer proposals:** each side sees the other's constraints and its own priorities side by side.
- **Better-fitting partners:** culturally aligned venues are suggested before cold outreach.

We have not run customer research and claim no time or money saved. The evaluation in `docs/evaluation.md`
measures correctness and grounding, not business impact.

## How it could be sustained

The product runs entirely on free tiers today. Paths that would not compromise its neutrality include a paid
tier for organisations that coordinate many small partnerships (markets, business improvement districts,
incubators) and white-label use by those organisations. Any such step would be a deliberate decision, not
something this repository enables by default.

## Out of scope

Payments, booking, automatic outreach, CRM features, revenue or traffic forecasts, and any inference of a
business's operations from cultural data.
